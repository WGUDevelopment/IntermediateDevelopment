The Language of Effective Coaching

How the words we choose can shift a conversation from providing answers to empowering students.

Teaching Is Telling. Coaching Is Exploring.

The language we use during a coaching session influences who is doing the thinking and who takes ownership of the learning process.

Teaching language focuses on delivering information, explaining concepts, demonstrating solutions, or telling students what they should do.

Coaching language focuses on asking thoughtful questions, encouraging reflection, and guiding students as they explore their own understanding and possible next steps.

Teaching: Telling

"Here's what you need to know."

The Peer Navigator directs the conversation by providing information or solutions.

Coaching: Exploring

"What do you already know?"

The Peer Navigator guides the conversation through questions that encourage the student to think.

Teaching gives students information. Coaching helps students develop their own understanding.

What Does This Sound Like in Practice?

Coaching language doesn't mean avoiding a student's question. It means responding in a way that keeps the student actively involved in the learning process.

Example 1: A Student Is Confused
Teaching Language

"Let me explain what that concept means."

Coaching Language

"Which part of the concept is making sense so far, and where are you getting stuck?"

Example 2: A Student Doesn't Know How to Start
Teaching Language

"First, you need to read this section and write down the key definitions."

Coaching Language

"What approach have you considered for getting started?"

Example 3: A Student Asks for an Answer
Teaching Language

"The correct answer is B because..."

Coaching Language

"Walk me through how you're approaching the question. What stands out to you?"

Remember: Coaching is not simply turning every statement into a question. The goal is to create opportunities for students to reflect, make decisions, and take ownership of their progress.
