Using the Feynman Technique to Aid Comprehension

Explore how the Feynman Technique can help students strengthen their comprehension, identify gaps in understanding, and build confidence in their ability to navigate course content independently.

1

Understanding the Feynman Technique

The Feynman Technique is a learning strategy that encourages students to explain a concept in its simplest terms. Rather than relying on memorized definitions or repeating information directly from the course material, students demonstrate their understanding by communicating the information in their own words.

When students struggle to simplify a concept, they have an opportunity to recognize where their understanding may be incomplete. They can then revisit their learning resources, clarify their understanding, and attempt to explain the concept again.

Benefits of the Feynman Technique

Strengthens Comprehension

Encourages students to process information beyond memorization and develop a deeper understanding of what they are learning.

Identifies Gaps in Understanding

Helps students recognize which concepts they understand confidently and which concepts require additional exploration.

Promotes Independent Learning

Encourages students to navigate their resources and work through confusion without relying on someone else to provide explanations.

Builds Student Confidence

Gives students an opportunity to recognize their own progress as they become more comfortable explaining complex information.

How the Strategy Works

1

Choose a Concept

Identify a concept or topic the student wants to better understand.

2

Explain Simply

Have the student explain the concept in their own words using simple language.

3

Identify Gaps

Recognize areas of uncertainty and revisit relevant learning resources.

4

Refine the Explanation

Explain the concept again using the student's strengthened understanding.

Maintaining the Coaching Lane

Your role as a Peer Navigator is to facilitate the use of the strategy, not to evaluate, confirm, or deny the accuracy of the student's explanation. When a student encounters a gap in understanding, encourage them to explore their course resources and refine their own explanation.

When asking questions, approach the conversation as someone who is trying to understand the student's explanation rather than someone who already knows the answer.

What to Pay Attention To

  • How the Peer Navigator introduces the strategy and establishes its purpose.
  • How the student is encouraged to explain information in their own words.
  • How questions are used to encourage the student to expand upon their explanation without turning the conversation into a content quiz.

What Is a Coaching Fishbowl?

A Coaching Fishbowl is a collaborative practice experience designed to provide Peer Navigators with opportunities to facilitate a simulated live event, observe their peers, and strengthen their coaching skills through shared feedback.

Each Coaching Fishbowl will consist of three Peer Navigators. One Peer Navigator will serve as the live event host, while the other two will participate as students.

The Development Program Supervisor or a Senior Peer Navigator will also attend each Coaching Fishbowl to facilitate the experience, help keep the session on track, and support the feedback conversation.

What makes this experience a Coaching Fishbowl rather than a traditional mock session is the opportunity for peer observation and feedback. At the conclusion of the simulated live event, the attendees will provide constructive feedback to the host, creating an opportunity for everyone to learn from the experience.

Participant Roles

Host
Serve as the live event facilitator. Introduce the Feynman Technique, engage participants in intentional practice, navigate content conversations appropriately, and facilitate reflection.
Attendees
Participate as students during the simulated live event. Actively engage in the strategy and provide constructive feedback to the host at the conclusion of the session.
Fishbowl Facilitator
The Development Program Supervisor or Senior Peer Navigator will provide structure, help keep the experience on track, and facilitate the peer feedback discussion.

Preparing for Your Coaching Fishbowl

When preparing to host your Coaching Fishbowl, you will use the Feynman Technique as your featured strategy and select any General Education (Gen Ed) course of your choice.

Prepare to facilitate the strategy as you would during a live event, keeping student engagement, independent learning, and appropriate redirection at the center of your approach.

Before Your Coaching Fishbowl
  • Select a General Education course.
  • Identify course content to use during intentional practice.
  • Review the Feynman Technique template.
  • Prepare to introduce and model the strategy.
  • Consider how you will navigate potential content-related challenges.

Coaching Fishbowl Session Structure

You will use a modified version of the AFFIRM framework when facilitating your Coaching Fishbowl. Because this experience is focused on strategy facilitation and navigating content conversations, you will skip Achievement Orientation and Moving Forward.

A

Achievement Orientation Skip

This step is not required during the Coaching Fishbowl.

F

Frame the Session

Establish the purpose of the session, introduce what participants can expect, and communicate expectations for participation.

F

Focus on the Strategy

Introduce the Feynman Technique, explain its purpose and benefits, and model how students can use the strategy to strengthen their comprehension.

I

Intentional Practice

Facilitate opportunities for participants to actively apply the Feynman Technique. Encourage participants to explain concepts in their own words, recognize gaps in understanding, and navigate their resources when additional exploration is needed.

R

Reflect and Review

Guide participants through reflection on their experience using the strategy. Explore what they learned, what they noticed about their comprehension, and how the strategy could support their independent learning.

M

Moving Forward Skip

This step is not required during the Coaching Fishbowl.

Peer Feedback

Following Reflect and Review, the simulated live event will conclude and the Fishbowl Facilitator will transition the group into a peer feedback discussion.

When participating as an attendee, consider the following questions as you prepare to provide feedback to the host:

  • How effectively did the host introduce and facilitate the strategy?
  • How did the host create opportunities for student engagement?
  • How did the host respond when participants expressed uncertainty or confusion?
  • How effectively did the host maintain the distinction between coaching and teaching?
  • What could the host continue doing or approach differently in future live events?
