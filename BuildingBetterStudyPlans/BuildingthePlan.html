Intermediate Development | Building Better Study Plans

Building the Plan Together

Helping students transform course material, available time, and learning goals into a realistic, actionable study plan through collaborative coaching.

From Course Content to a Clear Plan

Once you've recognized that a student could benefit from a study plan, the next step is helping them turn the course into a manageable roadmap. Rather than randomly approaching the course one section at a time without a clear direction, students can use a study plan to organize topics, establish weekly goals, identify available resources, and recognize when they may need additional support. A strong study plan isn't simply a schedule. It helps students think intentionally about what they'll study, when they'll study it, and how they'll approach their learning.

The most important distinction: We're not creating a plan for the student. We're facilitating a conversation that helps the student create a plan that works for them.

Introducing the Study Plan Template

The Peer Navigator Study Plan Template provides a structured way to help students organize their course into smaller, more manageable goals. The template organizes the course into weeks and individual study days. Students can identify which topics or sections they plan to review, additional resources they may use, and their progress toward completing each activity.

Each week also includes space for additional notes, such as upcoming Course Instructor appointments, Peer Navigator follow-ups, or topics that may require extra attention.

What Makes a Strong Study Plan?

An effective study plan connects the student's course goals with realistic actions, available resources, and ongoing opportunities to evaluate progress. As you collaborate with a student, consider how the following components might fit into their plan.

Component Purpose
Course Topics Break course material into manageable sections rather than approaching the course as one large task.
Weekly and Daily Goals Identify which topics the student intends to focus on during each week and individual study day.
Available Study Time Consider work schedules, family responsibilities, other commitments, and the time the student realistically has available.
Learning Strategies Explore how the student plans to learn and practice the material, not just which sections they'll read. Specific strategies can also be explored during future coaching appointments.
Course Resources Incorporate relevant videos, readings, practice activities, and other available resources. Students may choose to dedicate additional resources to unfamiliar or challenging topics.
Course Instructor Support Identify opportunities to schedule CI appointments and encourage students to keep a running list of questions or topics requiring clarification.
Live Cohorts and Events Include relevant learning opportunities that align with the student's course topics and support needs.
Progress Checks Build in opportunities to recognize progress, revisit priorities, and adjust the plan. Follow-up Peer Navigator appointments can support this reflection.
Completion Goal Connect weekly and daily activities to the student's intended course completion date or assessment goal.
Remember: The entire study plan does not need to be completed during the session. The goal is to help the student start their study plan, ask questions that will encourage reflection, and to make sure they leave with enough clarity to finish their study plan later on their own.

Stay in a Coaching Mindset, Not Advising

It's easy for a study-planning conversation to turn into an advising session. When we're familiar with a course or a particular planning strategy, we may be tempted to tell students exactly how much they should study, which topics to prioritize, or which resources to use. However, our role is to facilitate student decision-making, not make those decisions for them.

Advising: Directing

"Here's what you should do."

"You should finish two modules each week, study for two hours every evening, and schedule a CI appointment on Fridays."

The Peer Navigator decides what the plan should look like without exploring the student's circumstances.

Coaching: Collaborating

"What feels realistic for you?"

"Looking at your completion goal and the time you have available, how might you break these topics into manageable weekly goals?"

The Peer Navigator encourages the student to explore possibilities and make informed decisions.

Coaching doesn't mean withholding information about available resources or planning tools. We can introduce options, demonstrate how a template works, and help students consider possible approaches. The distinction is that the student maintains ownership of the decisions.

Study Time vs. Study Tasks

One important part of building an actionable study plan is helping students distinguish between setting aside study time and identifying what they'll actually do during that time.

A student might say they plan to study for two hours every evening. While that establishes a time commitment, it doesn't necessarily provide direction for how they'll use that time.

Turning a Vague Goal Into an Actionable Plan
Vague Study Goal

"Study Chapter 3 for two hours."

More Actionable Study Goal

"Review the Chapter 3 learning objectives, practice active recall on the major concepts, and identify questions to bring to my Course Instructor."

The second example provides a clearer sense of what the student intends to accomplish.

As a Peer Navigator, you can encourage students to make their goals more actionable without deciding which activities they must complete.

Try asking: "When you sit down to study this section, what would you like to accomplish during that time?"

"What activities do you think would help you work toward that goal?"

Students don't need to select a specific learning strategy for every study session while creating their initial plan. This can be a great opportunity to discuss scheduling future Peer Navigator appointments, where students can practice powerful learning strategies that align with the material they're currently studying.

Build Support Into the Plan

A study plan shouldn't rely entirely on independent studying. Students can also consider how the resources and support available to them might strengthen their learning.

Course Instructor Appointments

Students may find it helpful to schedule Course Instructor appointments after working through a section of material, allowing them to review what they've learned and address remaining questions. Encourage students to keep a running list of questions and areas of uncertainty as they study, so they can use their CI appointments intentionally.

Live Cohorts and Course Resources

Students can explore the additional resources available within their course and identify opportunities that align with their learning priorities. For example, a student who anticipates needing extra support with a particular module might choose to incorporate additional practice resources or relevant live cohorts into that week's plan.

Peer Navigator Follow-Ups

Future Peer Navigator appointments can provide opportunities to practice learning strategies, recognize progress, explore challenges, and adjust the study plan when needed. These conversations can also strengthen accountability by helping students reflect on the goals they've established and determine their next steps.

Important: These support options should be explored collaboratively. Rather than automatically scheduling or prescribing resources, help students decide which opportunities may be meaningful for them.

Adapting Study Planning to Your Coaching Setting

Study planning conversations can happen during both individual appointments and live events, but the approach may look different depending on the setting.

Individual Appointments

Individual appointments provide an opportunity to focus entirely on one student's goals, circumstances, and learning priorities. You can explore their available time, course familiarity, and preferences while helping them customize the study plan template. The entire appointment may be dedicated to creating the study plan, with future appointments used for accountability and strategy practice.

Live Events

Live events may include students with different course goals, levels of experience, schedules, and learning needs. Rather than attempting to create one plan for everyone, facilitate a guided planning experience that allows each student to make individual decisions. Introduce the template, model how to complete its sections, and use powerful questions to encourage reflection. Students can continue personalizing their plans after the event.

In a live event, the goal is not for every student to leave with a fully completed plan. Instead, focus on helping students understand how to use the planning process, recognize the decisions they need to make, and feel prepared to continue independently.

Another Tool: The Pomodoro Study Session Planner

Not every student needs a comprehensive plan for their entire course. Sometimes students have a clear understanding of what they need to study, but struggle to organize individual study sessions. In those situations, another tool in our Strategy Toolbox may be more appropriate: the Pomodoro Study Session Planner.

Bonus Strategy

Pomodoro Study Session Planner

The Pomodoro technique breaks study time into focused work intervals separated by short breaks.

Our planner helps students select a study goal, divide it into smaller tasks, and complete focused work sessions. The template uses four suggested 25-minute focus rounds, with 5-minute breaks between rounds and a longer break afterward. It also includes space for preparing to focus, recording distractions, and reflecting on what was accomplished. Students can adjust the work and break intervals to fit their needs.

When might it help? Consider a student who knows which chapter they need to work on but frequently gets distracted or feels disorganized whenever they sit down to study. Rather than developing an entire course plan, it may be more useful to help that student organize one productive study session.

Choosing the Right Tool

Although these two resources can support similar goals, they serve different purposes.

Study Plan Template

Planning the Course

Best suited for students who need a broader roadmap for organizing course topics, weekly goals, and academic support.

Pomodoro Planner

Planning a Study Session

Best suited for students who know what they need to study but want more structure, focus, and organization during individual study sessions.

Avoid information overload. You generally don't need to introduce both templates during the same coaching interaction. Consider which tool best fits the student's immediate need, and explore additional strategies later if appropriate.
